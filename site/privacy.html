Privacy Policy

What Jury Simulator does — and doesn't — collect.

Effective 7 May 2026 · Last updated 26 November 2026

Jury Simulator runs trials on your device. Most of what you do in the app — your voir dire answers, the conversations you have with jurors, your notes, your votes — never leaves your phone. This page explains the few exceptions and exactly what happens to that data.

What stays on your device

All trial dialogue is generated locally by an on-device model. That means:

  • Anything you type to a juror or write in your notebook is processed on the same phone you typed it on.
  • Your case history and verdicts are stored in a private local database that other apps cannot read.
  • Your juror profile (including the optional political-leaning field) is stored only on your device.
  • We do not have a server that holds your gameplay. There is nothing for us to leak, sell, or be subpoenaed for.
  • Android backup is disabled for this app, so your local data is not copied to cloud backups either.

All cases, people, and verdicts in the app are AI-generated fiction. If an AI message ever concerns you, you can long-press it in the app and choose Report this content.

What does leave your device

Model download

On first launch the app downloads the AI model (~3.65 GB) over HTTPS from Hugging Face, which hosts the model file (the Gemma 4 E4B IT LiteRT-LM build published by the litert-community project). This is a one-time download. We do not run a server in the middle — the request goes directly from your device to Hugging Face, which, like any web host, can see your IP address when you download. The download runs as a foreground service with a progress notification so it can finish even if you leave the app, and the app verifies the downloaded file's cryptographic hash (SHA-256) before using it. If you choose to supply your own model URL in the app, the download goes to that host instead, under the same rules.

AdMob ads

The app shows banner and occasional interstitial ads via Google AdMob, which supports development of the app. The Google Mobile Ads SDK may collect:

  • Your advertising ID and app set ID (the advertising ID is resettable in Android settings)
  • Approximate location (derived from your IP address)
  • App interactions (such as ad impressions and taps) and basic product-interaction events
  • Diagnostics, such as crash logs and app performance data
  • Device model, OS version, language, and similar technical data

Google uses this data for advertising, analytics, and fraud prevention, as described in Google's Ads & Privacy Policy and Google's AdMob data disclosure. Where the law requires it, the app asks for your consent before ads are requested. You can also limit personalized ads at any time in your Android device settings under Privacy → Ads → Reset advertising ID or Delete advertising ID. Jury Simulator will keep working — you'll just see less-relevant ads.

What we don't do

  • No third-party analytics of our own. No Firebase, no Crashlytics, no Mixpanel, no Amplitude — and this page itself loads no analytics trackers.
  • No account system. We don't ask you to sign up, sign in, or hand over an email.
  • No cross-app tracking by us. The only third parties involved are Hugging Face (the one-time model download) and Google AdMob (ads) — and neither sees your gameplay, chats, or notes, which never leave your device.

Children

Jury Simulator depicts criminal trials and is not intended for children under 13. We do not knowingly collect data from children. If you believe a child has been served personalized ads, you can disable ad personalization at the OS level (above) or uninstall the app.

Permissions, in plain language

  • Internet & network state — to download the model and load ads.
  • Foreground service & notifications — the one-time model download runs as a foreground service (type: data sync) with a progress notification, so it can complete even if you leave the app.

Changes to this policy

If we ever start collecting more data — for example, adding crash reporting — this page will be updated and the effective date above will change. The version of this policy that ships with each release is also archived in the public source repository.
